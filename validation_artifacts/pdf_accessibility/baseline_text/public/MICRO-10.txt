Consumer Surplus & Willingness to Pay
THE CORE IDEA
Consumer surplus is the difference between a buyer's willingness to pay and the price actually paid. Surplus
analysis compares value created with the opportunity cost of the resources used. Demand can be interpreted
as buyers' marginal willingness to pay. The gain from an additional trade is the buyer’s willingness to pay
minus the seller’s cost, not either side’s individual surplus alone.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Look for a buyer's willingness to pay and the market price actually paid.
•
Individual consumer surplus equals willingness to pay minus price.
•
On a graph, consumer surplus is the area below demand and above the market price up to the quantity
traded.
•
A buyer whose willingness to pay is below price does not purchase and earns no consumer surplus.
WATCH OUT
Do not assume every trade creates surplus. The gain from an additional trade is the buyer’s willingness
to pay minus the seller’s cost, not either side’s individual surplus alone.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CALCULATING CONSUMER SURPLUS
At equilibrium, price is $14 and quantity is 8. The
demand intercept of $30 represents the highest
willingness to pay. Consumer surplus is the triangle
above price and below demand: 1/2 x 8 x ($30 - $14)
= $64.
