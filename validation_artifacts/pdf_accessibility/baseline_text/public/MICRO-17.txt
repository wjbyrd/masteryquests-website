Gains from Trade, Surplus & Winners/Losers
THE CORE IDEA
International trade changes consumer and producer surplus and creates net gains when the world price
differs from the no-trade equilibrium price. The surplus framework converts distributional changes into a net
total-surplus comparison. Trade creates net gains while redistributing surplus between buyers and sellers.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Trade creates net gains while redistributing surplus between buyers and sellers.
•
The efficiency result is based on the net change in combined surplus, not on every group winning.
•
Higher export prices redistribute surplus toward producers while creating a positive net gain in the standard
model.
•
Aggregate gains do not imply universal gains.
WATCH OUT
Do not assume trade has only winners or only losers. Trade creates net gains while redistributing
surplus between buyers and sellers. The efficiency result is based on the net change in combined
surplus, not on every group winning.
WORKED EXAMPLE: TRACING TRADE WINNERS AND LOSERS
At the $30 world price, domestic producers supply 10
units and domestic consumers demand 50, so
imports equal 40. Relative to the $50 no-trade
equilibrium, the two gain-from-trade triangles each
have base 20 and height 20. Their combined area is
$200 + $200 = $400.
