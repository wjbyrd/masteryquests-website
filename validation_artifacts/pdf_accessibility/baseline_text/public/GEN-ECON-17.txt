Shortages and Surpluses
THE CORE IDEA
A shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied; a surplus occurs when quantity
supplied exceeds quantity demanded. A surplus occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
A shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied. Quantity demanded exceeds
quantity supplied, so there is a shortage. Shortages push price upward. A price above equilibrium creates a
surplus because quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
At the current price, buyers want 180 units and sellers offer 120 units.
•
A price above equilibrium creates a surplus because quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
•
A price below equilibrium creates a shortage because quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.
•
A surplus exists when quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to reverse shortage and price pressure. Quantity demanded exceeds quantity
supplied, so there is a shortage. Shortages push price upward.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MEASURING A SHORTAGE
At a price of $2 per gallon, the graph shows quantity
demanded of 200 thousand gallons and quantity
supplied of 100 thousand. Because buyers want 100
thousand more gallons than sellers offer, the market
has a shortage of 200 - 100 = 100 thousand gallons.
Upward pressure on price follows.
