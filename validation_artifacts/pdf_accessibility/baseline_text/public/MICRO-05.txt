Price Elasticity of Supply
THE CORE IDEA
Price elasticity of supply measures the percentage response of quantity supplied to a one-percent change in
price. Price Elasticity of Supply measures and interprets how quantity supplied responds to price changes,
including time horizon, capacity, storage, determinants, and midpoint calculations. Storage creates another
margin through which sellers can respond to price changes. Investment and capacity adjustment make
long-run supply more responsive. Price elasticity of supply compares the percentage change in quantity
supplied with the percentage change in the good’s price.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Look for a percentage change in quantity supplied caused by a percentage change in the good's price.
•
Compute elasticity as percentage change in quantity supplied divided by percentage change in price.
•
A larger elasticity means sellers respond more strongly to the price change.
•
Supply is usually more elastic when firms have more time or spare capacity to adjust production.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse supply elasticity with demand shifters. Price elasticity of supply compares the
percentage change in quantity supplied with the percentage change in the good’s price.
WORKED EXAMPLE: SHORT-RUN VERSUS LONG-RUN SUPPLY
The price rises from $4 to $8. On the short-run curve,
quantity rises from 60 to 70, so the midpoint quantity
change is 10/65 = 15.4%; the midpoint price change
is 4/6 = 66.7%. Short-run supply elasticity is
15.4%/66.7% = 0.23, which is inelastic. The flatter
long-run curve shows a larger quantity response.
CHECK YOURSELF
Using the midpoint method, the price of artisan bread loaves changes from $4 to $5 and quantity
supplied changes from 900 to 1080. What is price elasticity of supply?
