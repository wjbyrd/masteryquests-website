Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics
THE CORE IDEA
Microeconomics studies choices by individual households, firms, workers, and markets, while
macroeconomics studies economy-wide outcomes such as output, inflation, and unemployment. One event
can require both market-level and economy-wide analysis. Macro conclusions require appropriate
aggregation across people, firms, and markets. Macroeconomics examines economy-wide aggregates such
as output, inflation, and unemployment. A single firm's hiring decision is microeconomics.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A report tracks national inflation, unemployment, and total output.
•
A firm decides how much labor to hire at one restaurant.
•
Microeconomics can use massive data while focusing on individual choices and specific markets.
•
A bakery studies how customers respond to a muffin price increase.
•
Microeconomics studies choices by households, firms, and specific markets.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to label any large organization as macroeconomic. The classification depends on
the unit of analysis: one decision-maker is micro; economy-wide aggregates are macro.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MICRO OR MACRO?
A restaurant deciding whether to hire one more server is a microeconomic question because it concerns
one firm's choice. The national unemployment rate is a macroeconomic outcome because it aggregates
labor-market conditions across the economy. The subject's size is not decisive; the unit of analysis is.
