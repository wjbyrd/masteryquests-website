Oligopoly Structure, Strategic Interdependence & Concentration
THE CORE IDEA
An oligopoly contains only a few important firms, so each firm's decisions depend on expected rival
responses. Differentiation can exist in both; strategic few-firm response is distinctive. Dominant shares and
strategic interdependence point to oligopoly. With only a few important firms, one firm’s action can provoke
responses that change its own payoff.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Look for a market dominated by a few firms with meaningful barriers to entry.
•
Each firm's price, output, or advertising decision depends on how rivals are expected to respond.
•
Use concentration measures as evidence about structure, not as a complete verdict about competition.
•
The defining clue is strategic interdependence among the important firms.
WATCH OUT
Do not use firm count alone. Dominant shares and strategic interdependence point to oligopoly.
Concentration depends on the boundaries of the relevant market.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MEASURING MARKET CONCENTRATION
The graph shows market shares of 38%, 32%, 12%,
9%, and 9%. HHI equals the sum of squared shares:
38^2 + 32^2 + 12^2 + 9^2 + 9^2 = 1,444 + 1,024 +
144 + 81 + 81 = 2,774. That value indicates
substantial concentration.
