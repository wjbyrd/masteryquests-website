Short-Run Phillips Curve
THE CORE IDEA
The short-run Phillips curve shows an inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment when
expected inflation is fixed. With expected inflation initially fixed, a demand change moves the economy along
the current SRPC. The LRPC is vertical at the natural rate because expected inflation adjusts in the long run.
The short-run Phillips curve shows that lower unemployment is associated with higher inflation, and higher
unemployment is associated with lower inflation.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A short-run tradeoff between inflation and unemployment.
•
Higher AD raises output and prices, which maps to lower unemployment and higher inflation.
•
The short-run Phillips curve shows a trade-off between inflation and unemployment.
•
A move up and left along the displayed SRPC means lower unemployment and higher inflation.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse the Phillips curve with the AD-AS model. The short-run Phillips curve shows that lower
unemployment is associated with higher inflation, and higher unemployment is associated with lower
inflation.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MOVEMENT ALONG THE SRPC
Point A is at 5 percent unemployment and 2.5 percent
inflation. The horizontal guide through point A hits the
inflation axis at 2.5 percent. Therefore, 2.5 percent. A
downward-sloping SRPC shows an inverse short-run
relationship: less unemployment comes with more
inflation.
