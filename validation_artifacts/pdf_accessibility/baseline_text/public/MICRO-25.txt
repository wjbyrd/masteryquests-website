Sunk & Avoidable Costs
THE CORE IDEA
Sunk costs cannot be recovered and should not affect a current decision; avoidable costs change with the
option chosen. Past unrecoverable costs do not change the marginal consequences of the current decision.
The relevant comparison is forward-looking, not an attempt to reverse an unrecoverable past payment. The
current payment is sunk; the future renewal is avoidable and differs across options. Avoidable costs differ
across the alternatives being considered.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Ask whether a cost has already been incurred and cannot be recovered.
•
Exclude sunk costs from the comparison among current options.
•
Include avoidable costs because they differ depending on which option is chosen.
•
Focus on future benefits and future costs that change with the decision.
WATCH OUT
Do not treat fixed as sunk. The current payment is sunk; the future renewal is avoidable and differs
across options. Avoidable costs differ across the alternatives being considered.
WORKED EXAMPLE: IGNORE OR INCLUDE THE COST?
A theater has already paid a nonrefundable $4,000 license fee. Canceling tonight's show avoids $3,000
of staffing and utility costs but gives up $5,500 of ticket revenue. The sunk $4,000 does not affect the
current decision. Because operating adds $5,500 - $3,000 = $2,500, the theater should hold the show.
CHECK YOURSELF
A fixed annual license can be surrendered halfway through the year for a $4,000 refund. For a midyear
shutdown decision, which amount is relevant?
