Profit-Maximizing Output & the MR = MC Rule
THE CORE IDEA
A firm maximizes profit or minimizes loss by producing the quantity where rising marginal cost equals
marginal revenue. The local profit maximum occurs where MC rises through MR. Marginal benefit to the firm
exceeds marginal cost. Market price becomes the firm’s MR, linking market equilibrium to the firm’s marginal
output choice.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Use the firm's marginal-revenue curve and the rising portion of marginal cost.
•
Choose the output where marginal cost equals marginal revenue, provided the firm should operate.
•
If marginal revenue exceeds marginal cost, another unit raises profit; if marginal cost exceeds marginal
revenue, it lowers profit.
•
Do not choose output by minimizing average total cost unless that output also satisfies the marginal rule.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to misapply competitive firm rule. Market price becomes the firm’s MR, linking
market equilibrium to the firm’s marginal output choice.
WORKED EXAMPLE: USING THE MR = MC RULE
The firm's market price and marginal revenue are
$25. The rising marginal-cost curve reaches $25 at 50
units, so the last unit adds exactly as much revenue
as cost. Producing fewer units leaves profitable
output unmade; producing more makes MC exceed
MR. Profit-maximizing output is 50.
