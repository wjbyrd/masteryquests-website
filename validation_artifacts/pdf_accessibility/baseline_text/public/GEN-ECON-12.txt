Demand & the Law of Demand
THE CORE IDEA
Demand describes the quantities buyers are willing and able to purchase at different prices, holding other
demand conditions constant. Quantity demanded is the amount buyers choose at one particular price. The
law of demand states that a lower own price increases quantity demanded and a higher own price decreases
it, all else equal. An own-price change causes movement between points on the same demand curve; it does
not shift the curve.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
The good's own price changes while income, tastes, expectations, related-good prices, and the number of buyers
remain constant.
•
The question asks for quantity demanded at a particular price.
•
Two points lie on the same downward-sloping demand curve.
•
A lower price is paired with a larger quantity demanded.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to call every change in quantity a shift in demand. If the good's own price changes
and buyers move from one point to another on the same demand curve, quantity demanded changes.
Demand itself shifts only when a nonprice determinant changes.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MOVEMENT ALONG DEMAND
On the gourmet-donut graph, point A shows a price of $10 and
quantity demanded of 100 thousand. Point B shows a lower price
of $5 and quantity demanded of 225 thousand. Because both
points lie on the same demand curve and only the good's own
price changes, the move from A to B is an increase in quantity
demanded of 125 thousand, not an increase in demand.
CHECK YOURSELF
The price of a concert ticket falls and buyers move to another point on the same demand curve. What
changes: demand or quantity demanded?
