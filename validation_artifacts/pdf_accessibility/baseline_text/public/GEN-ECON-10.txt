Economists and Policy
THE CORE IDEA
Economists use evidence and models to estimate policy effects, identify tradeoffs, and explain uncertainty.
Responsible advice reports assumptions, uncertainty, and plausible unintended consequences. Economic
analysis clarifies tradeoffs but does not uniquely rank every social goal. Economists can estimate
consequences; recommendations also require value judgments. Economists analyze incentives, evidence,
and tradeoffs; elected officials make policy choices.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
An economist estimates a policy’s effects, then recommends adoption.
•
Economists analyze incentives, evidence, and tradeoffs; elected officials make policy choices.
•
Policy evaluation requires explicit objectives and evidence about consequences.
•
A policy model predicts large benefits, but the data come from a different population and period.
•
Good advice makes assumptions, tradeoffs, and uncertainty visible.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to give economists authority to choose society's values. Economists can estimate
consequences and tradeoffs; the policy choice also requires normative judgments.
WORKED EXAMPLE: EVALUATING POLICY ADVICE
An economist estimates that a proposed transit subsidy will increase ridership by 8%, but the estimate
comes from a city with denser development. Professional advice reports the predicted benefit, explains
the uncertain transfer to the local setting, and identifies fiscal costs. Whether those consequences justify
the policy also depends on public values and priorities.
