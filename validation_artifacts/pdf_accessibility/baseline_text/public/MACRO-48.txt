THE CORE IDEA
The debt level is a nominal dollar stock; the debt-to-GDP ratio compares that stock with the economy's annual income and productive
capacity. Ratios support comparisons across time and economies, but they do not by themselves prove sustainability. Interest rates,
economic growth, revenues, primary balances, maturity, and investor confidence also matter at a Principles level.
?
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
-
Debt-to-GDP = public debt divided by nominal GDP, multiplied by 100 percent.
-
Debt can rise while debt-to-GDP falls if nominal GDP grows faster.
-
Interest burden can be compared with GDP, revenue, or total outlays.
-
Nominal levels answer size questions; ratios answer size-relative-to-capacity questions.
!
WATCH OUT
Do not interpret one ratio as a mechanical crisis threshold. Use consistent definitions and dates, then combine the ratio with growth,
interest costs, and budget information.
