Short-Run Cost Curves: Relationships & Shifts
THE CORE IDEA
Short-run cost curves move differently because fixed costs affect AFC and ATC, while variable-input costs
affect AVC, ATC, and MC. A below-average marginal observation pulls the average down. Labor is the
variable input in the standard short-run model. A fixed-cost increase raises fixed cost per unit and total cost
per unit but leaves variable and marginal cost unchanged.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A fixed-cost increase raises fixed cost per unit and total cost per unit but leaves variable and marginal cost
unchanged.
•
A technology improvement reduces the variable input needed at every output.
•
Labor is the variable input in the standard short-run model.
•
A firm receives a property-tax increase that does not depend on output.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to mix fixed and variable shifts. A fixed-cost increase raises fixed cost per unit and
total cost per unit but leaves variable and marginal cost unchanged.
WORKED EXAMPLE: SHIFTING SHORT-RUN COST CURVES
Suppose the firm signs a more expensive lease while
output and variable input prices are unchanged. The
higher fixed cost raises average fixed cost and
therefore shifts ATC upward. AVC and MC remain in
the positions shown because neither depends on
fixed cost. The production decision at the margin is
unchanged.
