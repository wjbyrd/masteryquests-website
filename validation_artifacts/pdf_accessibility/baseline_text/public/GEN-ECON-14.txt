Supply & the Law of Supply
THE CORE IDEA
Supply describes the quantities sellers are willing and able to offer at different prices, holding other supply
conditions constant. Quantity supplied is the amount sellers offer at one particular price. The law of supply states
that a higher own price generally increases quantity supplied and a lower own price decreases it, all else equal.
An own-price change causes movement between points on the same supply curve; it does not shift the curve.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
The good's own price changes while production conditions remain constant.
•
The question asks for quantity supplied at a particular price.
•
Two points lie on the same upward-sloping supply curve.
•
A higher price is paired with a larger quantity supplied.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to call every change in quantity a shift in supply. If the good's own price changes
and sellers move from one point to another on the same supply curve, quantity supplied changes.
Supply itself shifts only when a nonprice determinant changes.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MOVEMENT ALONG SUPPLY
On the coffee supply graph, point A shows a price of $6 and
quantity supplied of 100 thousand. Point B shows a higher price of
$11 and quantity supplied of 225 thousand. Because both points lie
on the same supply curve and only coffee's own price changes, the
move from A to B is an increase in quantity supplied of 125
thousand, not an increase in supply.
