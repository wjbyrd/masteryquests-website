Price Floors
THE CORE IDEA
A price floor sets a legal minimum price and is binding only when it lies above the market equilibrium price. A
binding floor keeps price from falling to equilibrium, so quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded and a
surplus develops. A floor at or below equilibrium is nonbinding because the market can still reach its
equilibrium price.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Compare the legal minimum price with the market equilibrium price.
•
A floor above equilibrium is binding and creates excess supply.
•
At the floor price, read quantity supplied and quantity demanded; their difference is the surplus.
•
A floor at or below equilibrium is nonbinding and does not change the market outcome.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to assume that every price floor is binding. Equilibrium is $30. A floor at $20 is
below equilibrium, so it does not prevent the market from reaching $30.
WORKED EXAMPLE: BINDING PRICE FLOOR
The market equilibrium is $30 and 200 units. A
binding floor of $40 holds price above equilibrium;
sellers offer 250 units while buyers demand 150. The
resulting surplus is 250 - 150 = 100 units, so some
output remains unsold at the controlled price.
