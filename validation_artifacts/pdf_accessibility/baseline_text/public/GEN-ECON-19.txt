Price Ceilings
THE CORE IDEA
A price ceiling sets a legal maximum price and is binding only when it lies below the market equilibrium price.
A binding ceiling keeps price from rising to equilibrium, so quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied and
a shortage develops. A ceiling at or above equilibrium is nonbinding because the market can still reach its
equilibrium price.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Compare the legal maximum price with the market equilibrium price.
•
A ceiling below equilibrium is binding and creates excess demand.
•
At the ceiling price, read quantity demanded and quantity supplied; their difference is the shortage.
•
A ceiling at or above equilibrium is nonbinding and does not change the market outcome.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to assume that every price ceiling is binding. A price ceiling above equilibrium is
not binding. Since $40 is above the $30 equilibrium price, it does not affect the market.
WORKED EXAMPLE: BINDING PRICE CEILING
The market equilibrium is $30 and 200 units. A
binding ceiling of $20 holds price below equilibrium;
buyers demand 250 units while sellers supply 150.
The resulting shortage is 250 - 150 = 100 units, so
some willing buyers cannot purchase at the controlled
price.
