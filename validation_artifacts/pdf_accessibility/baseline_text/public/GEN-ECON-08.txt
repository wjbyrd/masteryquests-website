Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF)
THE CORE IDEA
A production possibilities frontier shows the combinations of two goods an economy can produce with its
current resources and technology. A bowed-out PPF shows increasing opportunity cost as resources are
shifted toward one good. Opportunity cost rises because resources are specialized and become less well
suited as they are shifted into the other good. A PPF shows possible production combinations given current
resources and technology.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A PPF shows possible production combinations given current resources and technology.
•
A new technology improves only medical production.
•
An economy first removes unemployment, then gains new machinery.
•
Using idle resources changes the production point; resources or technology shift the frontier.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse a PPF with a market curve. A PPF shows possible production combinations given
current resources and technology. Using idle resources changes the production point; resources or
technology shift the frontier.
WORKED EXAMPLE: READING A PPF
On the PPF, moving from point C (15 units of X and
35 of Y) to point D (21 of X and 20 of Y) gains 6 units
of X but gives up 15 units of Y. The total opportunity
cost is 15 units of Y, or 15/6 = 2.5 units of Y per
additional unit of X.
