Monopoly Welfare, Efficiency & Competitive Comparison
THE CORE IDEA
Compared with competition, monopoly usually restricts output, raises price, transfers surplus, and creates
deadweight loss. Transfers remain in total surplus; unrealized trades do not. The omitted units would
generate positive gains from trade. Market power generally leads the monopolist to restrict output and charge
a higher price than the competitive benchmark.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Compare monopoly output and price with the competitive benchmark where price equals marginal cost.
•
Monopoly normally produces less and charges more than a competitive market.
•
Separate the transfer of surplus to the monopolist from surplus lost through reduced output.
•
Deadweight loss is the value of trades between monopoly output and the efficient quantity that no longer
occur.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to reverse monopoly competitive comparison. Market power generally leads the
monopolist to restrict output and charge a higher price than the competitive benchmark.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MEASURING MONOPOLY DEADWEIGHT LOSS
The unregulated monopoly chooses about 45 units
where MR = MC and charges the demand price of
about $47. The efficient outcome occurs where
demand meets MC: 90 units at $20. Monopoly
therefore restricts output and raises price, leaving
mutually beneficial units between 45 and 90
unproduced.
