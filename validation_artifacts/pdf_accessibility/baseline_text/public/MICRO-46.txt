Advertising, Branding & Nonprice Competition
THE CORE IDEA
Advertising can provide information, persuade buyers, build a brand, and change the elasticity or position of a
firm's demand curve. The firm is competing through product features and service rather than changing price.
In signaling models, willingness to incur a visible selling cost can sometimes convey information when higher
quality supports repeat sales. A fixed selling cost raises fixed and average total cost but does not directly
raise MC or AVC. The ad is trying to shape preferences and brand perception rather than mainly transmit
facts.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A shoe company introduces custom colors and faster delivery while leaving its posted price unchanged.
•
Consumers cannot directly observe product durability before purchase.
•
A firm buys a one-time advertising campaign that does not change demand.
•
A perfume ad links the brand with status and excitement but provides almost no factual product information.
•
A branding campaign leaves the number of buyers unchanged but makes them less price sensitive.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to misapply monopolistic competition rule. The firm is competing through product
features and service rather than changing price. In signaling models, willingness to incur a visible selling
cost can sometimes convey information when higher quality supports repeat sales.
WORKED EXAMPLE: INFORMATIVE OR PERSUASIVE ADVERTISING?
A pharmacy advertises that a prescription costs $8 and that it stays open until 10 p.m. Buyers can
compare stores more easily, so search costs fall; this is informational advertising. A separate campaign
associates the brand with status without adding product facts; that campaign is persuasive advertising.
Both compete without directly cutting price.
