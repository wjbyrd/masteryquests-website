Shutdown & Short-Run Loss Minimization
THE CORE IDEA
A firm should operate in the short run when price covers average variable cost, even if price is below average
total cost. A lump-sum tax acts like fixed cost. AVC represents avoidable cost in the short run. Short-run loss
minimization compares the incremental benefit and avoidable cost of operating.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Find the profit-maximizing or loss-minimizing output where marginal revenue equals rising marginal cost.
•
At that output, compare price with average variable cost.
•
Operate in the short run when price covers average variable cost; shut down when price falls below it.
•
A firm may operate at a loss when it covers variable cost and contributes something toward fixed cost.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to misapply competitive firm rule. Short-run loss minimization compares the
incremental benefit and avoidable cost of operating. A fixed-cost change affects profit and ATC but not
MC or AVC, so it does not change the marginal output choice or shutdown threshold.
WORKED EXAMPLE: SHUT DOWN OR OPERATE?
The horizontal price line is $10, below minimum AVC
of $20 at point A. At every positive output, revenue
per unit would be less than avoidable variable cost.
The firm minimizes its short-run loss by shutting down
and paying only fixed cost.
