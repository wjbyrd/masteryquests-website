Short-Run Production & Diminishing Marginal Product
THE CORE IDEA
In the short run, at least one input is fixed, so output changes as additional units of a variable input are
employed. Input flexibility is the production-side basis for distinguishing fixed and variable cost. The same
arithmetic relationship governs MP versus AP and MC versus AVC or ATC.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Production case 2: Ironwood Furniture has MP sequence 6, 10, 13, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4.
•
Production case 4: Cedar Trail Bikes has MP sequence 9, 12, 13, 11, 9, 6, 2, -2.
•
Production case 6: Riverbend Textiles has MP sequence 11, 14, 15, 13, 11, 9, 7, 5.
•
Production case 8: Summit Glassworks has MP sequence 12, 15, 16, 14, 12, 10, 8, 6.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to wait until mp negative. Identify the first decline in MP and separate it from
negative MP and long-run scale.
WORKED EXAMPLE: DIMINISHING MARGINAL PRODUCT
Total product rises from 34 units with three workers to
46 with four, so the fourth worker's marginal product is
12. The fifth worker raises output from 46 to 56, giving
marginal product of 10. Output still increases, but the
smaller addition shows diminishing marginal product.
