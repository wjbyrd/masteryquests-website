Changes in Surplus & Policy Effects
THE CORE IDEA
A price or policy change can transfer surplus between buyers and sellers and can destroy surplus by
preventing mutually beneficial trades. A transfer changes who receives existing gains without changing their
total. Deadweight loss is different: it represents gains from exchange that disappear because quantity moves
away from the efficient level.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Establish the initial consumer surplus, producer surplus, and total surplus before the change.
•
Recalculate those areas after the price change or policy intervention.
•
Separate transfers between buyers and sellers from surplus that disappears entirely.
•
Deadweight loss represents mutually beneficial trades prevented by the change.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to count only existing buyers or only new buyers. With the same trades still
occurring, the price change transfers surplus dollar for dollar from sellers to buyers; it does not change
total surplus.
WORKED EXAMPLE: TRANSFER OR DEADWEIGHT LOSS?
At price P1 = $12, quantity is 8 and consumer surplus
is 1/2 x 8 x ($20 - $12) = $32. At P2 = $8, quantity
rises to 12 and consumer surplus becomes 1/2 x 12 x
($20 - $8) = $72. The price decrease raises consumer
surplus by $40.
