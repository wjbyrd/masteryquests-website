Total Surplus & Gains from Exchange
THE CORE IDEA
Total surplus equals consumer surplus plus producer surplus and measures the gains created by exchange.
Each trade adds to total surplus when the buyer's willingness to pay exceeds the seller's opportunity cost.
Total surplus is maximized when every mutually beneficial trade occurs and no trade with a cost above its
value is completed.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Add consumer surplus and producer surplus to obtain total surplus.
•
For each possible trade, compare the buyer's willingness to pay with the seller's opportunity cost.
•
A trade creates gains when buyer value exceeds seller cost.
•
On a graph, total surplus is the area between demand and supply up to the quantity traded.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to fail to separate transfer from total loss. Consumer surplus is $10 and producer
surplus is $8; their sum equals the $18 gain from exchange.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CALCULATING TOTAL SURPLUS
The graph's equilibrium is 8 units at $14. Consumer
surplus is 1/2 x 8 x ($30 - $14) = $64, and producer
surplus is 1/2 x 8 x ($14 - $6) = $32. Total surplus is
therefore $64 + $32 = $96.
