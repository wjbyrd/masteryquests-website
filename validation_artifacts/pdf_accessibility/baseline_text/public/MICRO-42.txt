Price Discrimination
THE CORE IDEA
Price discrimination charges different buyers different prices for the same product when resale can be
prevented. Preventing resale is what allows separated prices to persist. Different demand responsiveness is
the economic reason separated groups can receive different optimal prices. Charging each unit up to
willingness to pay can make serving all units with value at least MC profitable. Price discrimination requires
control over price and a way to distinguish relevant buyers or markets.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Charging each unit up to willingness to pay can make serving all units with value at least MC profitable.
•
Price discrimination requires control over price and a way to distinguish relevant buyers or markets.
•
Perfect discrimination can expand output to the efficient quantity while transferring surplus to the seller.
•
The firm allocates output across separated markets using marginal revenue relative to marginal cost.
•
Different demand responsiveness is the economic reason separated groups can receive different optimal
prices.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to disconnect wtp from first degree efficiency. Charging each unit up to willingness
to pay can make serving all units with value at least MC profitable.
WORKED EXAMPLE: TESTING PRICE DISCRIMINATION
A theater charges students $8 and other adults $14 for the same seat. The policy can persist only if
students cannot resell discounted tickets to adults; otherwise arbitrage would erase the price difference.
Verification separates the groups, and the different prices reflect different demand elasticities.
