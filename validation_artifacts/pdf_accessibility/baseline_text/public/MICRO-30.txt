Profit, Loss & Break-Even
THE CORE IDEA
Economic profit or loss equals the gap between price and average total cost multiplied by output. Profit, Loss
& Break-Even measures per-unit and total economic profit or loss and interpret break-even and zero
economic profit. The firm combines its revenue from price and quantity with all economic costs to measure
profit. Profit/loss measurement and the short-run shutdown decision use different comparisons. Separate
output, operating status, profit or loss, and long-run entry or exit pressure.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
First find the firm's chosen output using the marginal-revenue and marginal-cost rule.
•
At that output, compare price with average total cost.
•
Price above average total cost means profit; equality means break-even; price below average total cost
means loss.
•
Measure profit or loss as the per-unit gap multiplied by output.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse loss with shutdown. Profit/loss measurement and the short-run shutdown decision use
different comparisons. Economic cost includes implicit opportunity costs, including the normal return
needed to keep owner resources in the firm.
WORKED EXAMPLE: PROFIT, LOSS, OR BREAK-EVEN?
The market sets a price of $15. The representative
firm produces 50 units where P = MR = MC, but its
ATC is $18. Loss per unit is $18 - $15 = $3, so total
loss is $3 x 50 = $150. The firm may still operate
because price exceeds AVC.
