Fiscal Multipliers and Crowding Out
THE CORE IDEA
A spending or tax change can create a multiplied demand response, while higher interest rates can crowd out part of
the expansion. Fiscal policy can shift AD right, the multiplier can push it farther, and crowding out can pull the final
effect back. Crowding out offsets fiscal expansion by reducing private investment when interest rates rise. Crowding out
reduces private investment when fiscal expansion raises interest rates. In the simple fixed-price model with lump-sum
taxes and no import leakage, the spending multiplier is 1 / (1 - MPC), and the tax multiplier is -MPC / (1 - MPC).
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Initial fiscal shift, multiplier expansion, then crowding out offset.
•
Crowding out offsets fiscal expansion by reducing private investment when interest rates rise.
•
Crowding out reduces private investment when fiscal expansion raises interest rates.
•
Crowding out occurs when higher interest rates reduce private investment spending.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to report a gross multiplier effect without subtracting private spending displaced
by higher interest rates. Crowding out makes the net demand increase smaller.
WORKED EXAMPLE: MULTIPLIER VERSUS CROWDING OUT
With MPC = 0.8, the simple spending multiplier is 5. A $20 billion
increase in purchases raises demand by $100 billion before
offsets. In the graph, AD1 to AD3 is the gross expansion;
crowding out leaves AD2. At fixed P, real output demanded rises
to Y2 rather than Y3. Actual equilibrium output also depends on
aggregate supply.
