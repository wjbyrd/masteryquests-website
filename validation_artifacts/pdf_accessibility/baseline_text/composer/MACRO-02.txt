GDP Components
THE CORE IDEA
The expenditure approach divides GDP into consumption, investment, government purchases, and net
exports. Consumption covers household purchases of final goods and services; investment includes
business capital, residential construction, and inventory changes; government purchases pay for current
goods and services. Net exports add exports and subtract imports so GDP counts only domestic production,
while transfer payments themselves are excluded. A recipient's later purchase is counted separately if it pays
for current domestic output.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Classify final spending as consumption, investment, government purchases, or net exports.
•
Count newly produced capital goods and inventory changes as investment.
•
Exclude transfer payments because they are not payments for current production.
•
Subtract imports within net exports so foreign production is not counted in domestic GDP.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to count transfer payments as government purchases. Transfers redistribute
income and are never themselves part of GDP. A later purchase is classified by what is bought; imports
are subtracted in NX.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CLASSIFYING GDP EXPENDITURES
A business buys a newly produced domestic machine for $2 million; this is investment. Government
pays contractors $80 million to repair roads; this is government purchases. An $80 million benefit
payment is a transfer and is not counted when sent, though any later household purchase of final goods
enters consumption. The expenditure identity classifies the counted spending as C + I + G + NX.
