Models and Assumptions
THE CORE IDEA
Economic models simplify reality to isolate the relationships that matter for a particular question. The
two-good abstraction exposes scarcity and opportunity cost without describing every output. Evaluate a
model by its purpose, assumptions, performance, and relevant context. Extra complexity is useful only when
its benefits exceed lost clarity or robustness. Apply a model where its important assumptions remain
reasonable.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Adding dozens of variables improves historical fit but makes the mechanism impossible to interpret.
•
A central bank changes its policy rule, and a forecasting relationship estimated under the old rule breaks
down.
•
A local haircut model omits exchange rates and global shipping.
•
An economist builds a simple two-market circular-flow model and leaves out banks, government, and
foreign trade.
•
An economist studies how price affects purchases while holding income constant.
WATCH OUT
Do not use the model without checking its purpose, assumptions, or evidence. Evaluate a model by its
purpose, assumptions, performance, and relevant context.
WORKED EXAMPLE: TESTING A MODEL
A PPF model includes only two goods, even though real economies produce thousands. That
simplification isolates scarcity and opportunity cost: moving toward more of one good requires less of
the other. The model is useful for that purpose, but it would be inadequate for predicting the output of a
specific industry without additional detail.
