Real versus Nominal GDP
THE CORE IDEA
Nominal GDP values current production at current prices, while real GDP values it at base-year prices.
Multiply current quantities by the designated base-year prices and add across final goods. GDP is a strong
measure of market production for its purpose, but it omits some dimensions of welfare. Compare nominal and
real GDP to separate price changes from changes in production. Real-GDP growth equals the change in real
GDP divided by the initial real GDP.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Nominal GDP uses current-year prices and current-year quantities.
•
Real GDP uses base-year prices with current-year quantities.
•
A change in nominal GDP can reflect prices, quantities, or both; a change in real GDP reflects quantities.
•
Use the GDP deflator to compare nominal GDP with real GDP for the same year.
WATCH OUT
Do not use the new level rather than the initial real-GDP level as the percentage-growth denominator.
Real-GDP growth equals the change in real GDP divided by the initial real GDP.
WORKED EXAMPLE: SEPARATING PRICE AND OUTPUT CHANGES
An economy produces 100 units in the base year at $5 each and 110 units this year at $6 each. Nominal
GDP this year is 110 x $6 = $660. Real GDP at base-year prices is 110 x $5 = $550. The GDP deflator
is ($660 / $550) x 100 = 120, separating the price increase from the output increase.
