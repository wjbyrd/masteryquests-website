Product Variety, Efficiency & Model Limits
THE CORE IDEA
Monopolistic competition trades some cost and allocative efficiency for product variety and differentiated
consumer choices. Variety may create utility even while markup and excess capacity remain. Variety can
create utility even with efficiency costs. Differentiation can create consumer value through variety even when
the standard model shows some productive inefficiency.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Variety may create utility even while markup and excess capacity remain.
•
Variety can create utility even with efficiency costs.
•
Differentiation can create consumer value through variety even when the standard model shows some
productive inefficiency.
•
Two markets have the same total production cost.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to misapply monopolistic competition rule. Differentiation can create consumer
value through variety even when the standard model shows some productive inefficiency.
WORKED EXAMPLE: VARIETY VERSUS EFFICIENCY
The long-run firm produces 36 units at a price and
ATC of $27, so economic profit is zero. Marginal cost
is only $15, meaning price exceeds MC, and output
lies left of minimum ATC. Product variety may benefit
consumers, but the model also predicts markup and
excess capacity.
