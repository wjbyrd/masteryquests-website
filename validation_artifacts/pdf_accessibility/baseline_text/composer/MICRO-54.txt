THE CORE IDEA
An externality is a cost or benefit imposed on someone outside a transaction. Negative externalities make private decision makers
ignore part of social cost; positive externalities make them ignore part of social benefit. Efficiency uses all marginal social costs and
benefits.
?
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
-
Ask whether the spillover is a cost or benefit and whether it comes from production or consumption.
-
Negative: MSC > MPC or MPB > MSB, so the market quantity is too high.
-
Positive: MSB > MPB or MPC > MSC, so the market quantity is too low.
-
Corrective taxes, subsidies, permits, regulation, or bargaining can internalize the spillover.
!
WATCH OUT
Do not label every harmful outcome an externality. A spillover must affect a third party and be missing from the market price.
#
WORKED EXAMPLE: NEGATIVE PRODUCTION EXTERNALITY
Fast-fashion production creates a $6 external cost per
garment, so MSC lies $6 above MPC. The market
produces 240 million garments where MPB = MPC at
$12. Efficiency occurs at 160 million garments where
MPB = MSC at $15, eliminating 80 million units of
overproduction. A $6 corrective tax aligns private and
social marginal cost.
?
CHECK YOURSELF
If MSB lies above MPB, is the unregulated market quantity too high or too low?
