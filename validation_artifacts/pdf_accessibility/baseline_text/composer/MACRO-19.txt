Central Banking and the Federal Reserve
THE CORE IDEA
The Federal Reserve conducts monetary policy, supplies reserve balances, supports payments, and serves as
lender of last resort. Changes in reserves and interest rates are monetary policy territory. The Fed acts as the
central bank and lender of last resort. The Federal Reserve conducts monetary policy and supports the
banking/payments system; the Treasury manages federal finances and debt operations. Commercial banks create
deposit liabilities when they lend; the central bank creates reserve balances/base money used by banks. The Fed
includes the Board of Governors and 12 regional Reserve Banks. The FOMC directs monetary policy. Operational
independence allows policy decisions without day-to-day political approval, within a mandate set by Congress.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
The Federal Reserve conducts monetary policy and supports the banking/payments system; the Treasury
manages federal finances and debt operations.
•
The FOMC sets the monetary-policy stance and directs open-market operations.
•
A central-bank committee decides to change monetary conditions.
•
A commercial bank approves a loan and credits the borrower’s checking account.
•
Commercial banks create deposit liabilities when they lend; the central bank creates reserve balances/base
money used by banks.
WATCH OUT
Do not treat commercial-bank deposits and central-bank reserves as the same liability issued by the
same institution. Commercial banks create deposit liabilities when they lend; the central bank creates
reserve balances/base money used by banks.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CENTRAL BANK OR TREASURY?
The Federal Reserve buys Treasury securities from a bank, adding reserve balances. In the
limited-reserves textbook model, this tends to lower short-term rates; in an ample-reserves system,
administered rates are the main rate-control tool. The purchase is monetary policy, not a change in
federal taxes or spending.
