Long-Run Industry Supply & Cost Conditions
THE CORE IDEA
Long-run industry supply depends on how industry expansion changes firms' input costs. The long-run
industry response can feed back through shared input markets. The slope depends on how industry
expansion affects input costs and firms' cost curves.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
In a constant-cost competitive industry, industry expansion leaves input prices and firms’ cost curves
essentially unchanged.
•
A learner interprets a downward-sloping long-run industry supply curve as a violation of competition.
•
Short-run market adjustment creates the profit signal; entry and cost conditions determine the long-run
destination.
•
Read only the relationships displayed and apply the competitive-firm sequence in the correct order.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse industry cost conditions. If expansion does not change input costs, the long-run
equilibrium price can return to the original cost level.
WORKED EXAMPLE: READING LONG-RUN INDUSTRY SUPPLY
The long-run industry supply curve slopes upward,
rising from roughly $18 at very low output to above
$50 near 105 units. Industry expansion therefore
raises the long-run equilibrium price. That pattern is
consistent with an increasing-cost industry in which
entry bids up specialized inputs or other production
costs.
