THE CORE IDEA
A market failure occurs when private decisions do not produce an efficient allocation of resources. An
externality affects people outside the transaction. Public goods are nonrival and nonexcludable; common
resources are rival and hard to exclude. Identify the wedge between private incentives and social value or
cost. Public goods are characterized by nonrivalry and nonexcludability.

WATCH OUT
Do not confuse government provision with the economic definition of a public good. Public goods are
characterized by nonrivalry and nonexcludability. Identify the wedge between private incentives and
social value or cost.
WORKED EXAMPLE: IDENTIFYING AN EXTERNALITY
A factory's production imposes $8 of pollution damage per unit on nearby residents, but the factory pays
only its $20 private marginal cost. Social marginal cost is $20 + $8 = $28. If buyers' marginal benefit is
$24, the unit looks worthwhile privately but destroys $4 of social surplus, demonstrating an external-cost
market failure.
