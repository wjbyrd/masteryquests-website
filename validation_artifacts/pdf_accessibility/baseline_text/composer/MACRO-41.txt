Sacrifice Ratio
THE CORE IDEA
The sacrifice ratio measures cumulative output lost for each percentage-point reduction in inflation. Credibility
can speed expectations adjustment, but disinflation costs depend on model assumptions and wage-price
adjustment. The preferred path depends on credibility, expectations, adjustment speed, uncertainty, and
cumulative costs. The sacrifice ratio measures how much output is lost when inflation is reduced. If expected
inflation falls faster, the short-run cost of disinflation can be smaller.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
The sacrifice ratio measures how much output is lost when inflation is reduced.
•
If expected inflation falls faster, the short-run cost of disinflation can be smaller.
•
The sacrifice ratio measures cumulative output loss per percentage-point reduction in inflation.
•
A disinflation has a lower sacrifice ratio than another.
•
Faster adjustment of expected inflation reduces the output cost of disinflation.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to divide the dollar output loss directly by the inflation change. First express the
loss as a percentage of potential output, then divide by the percentage-point reduction in inflation.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CALCULATING THE SACRIFICE RATIO
Potential output is $400 billion and cumulative output loss during disinflation is $24 billion. The loss is
$24 / $400 = 6% of potential output. Inflation falls by 3 percentage points, so the sacrifice ratio is 6 / 3 =
2: the economy loses 2% of one year's output per percentage-point reduction in inflation.
