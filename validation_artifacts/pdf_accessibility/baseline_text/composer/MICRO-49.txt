Game Theory Foundations: Payoff Matrices, Best Responses & Nash Equilibrium
THE CORE IDEA
Game theory represents strategic choices with players, actions, and payoffs that depend on what others do.
Correctly reading the matrix is the prerequisite for identifying best responses and equilibrium. Compare
mutual best responses with the combined-payoff maximum. A Nash equilibrium is a cell containing mutual
best responses.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Identify the players, each player's available actions, and the payoff associated with every action
combination.
•
For each possible rival action, mark the action that gives a player the highest payoff.
•
A dominant strategy is best regardless of the rival's action.
•
A Nash equilibrium is a cell where every player's action is a best response to the others.
WATCH OUT
Do not assume equilibrium is efficient. Compare mutual best responses with the combined-payoff
maximum. A Nash equilibrium is a cell containing mutual best responses.
WORKED EXAMPLE: FINDING A NASH EQUILIBRIUM
For each strategy of the other player, mark the action
that gives a player the larger payoff. In the displayed
matrix, row A against column X gives (9, 9), while
switching unilaterally to row B would lower the row
player's payoff from 9 to 3. The column player also
prefers X to Y when row A is chosen, so (A, X) is a
cell of mutual best responses and is a Nash
equilibrium.
