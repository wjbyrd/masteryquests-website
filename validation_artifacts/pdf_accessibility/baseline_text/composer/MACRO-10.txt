Living Standards and Growth
THE CORE IDEA
Real GDP per person is a common measure of material living standards, and compounding makes sustained
growth powerful over time. Dividing real output by population distinguishes growth in total production from
growth in average material resources. Productivity growth can raise output per person without requiring
proportionally more work, although this measure does not capture every dimension of well-being.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A country raises output mainly by requiring much longer workweeks, while output per hour is unchanged.
•
Higher productivity lets an economy produce more goods and services from its available labor and
resources.
•
Real GDP per person is commonly used to compare living standards and growth.
•
Real GDP per person relates inflation-adjusted output to the number of people sharing that output.
•
Compare growth in real GDP with population growth to distinguish total output from output per person.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to equate more total labor input with higher productivity or complete welfare
improvement. Productivity is output per unit of input, and GDP omits important dimensions of well-being.
WORKED EXAMPLE: COMPOUNDING LIVING-STANDARD GROWTH
If real GDP per person grows at 4% per year, the Rule of 70 estimates a doubling time of 70/4 = 17.5
years. This calculation describes material output growth, not complete well-being: longer work hours
can raise GDP per person without raising output per hour or preserving leisure.
