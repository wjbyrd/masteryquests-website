Fiscal Policy and Aggregate Demand
THE CORE IDEA
Fiscal policy shifts aggregate demand through changes in government purchases, taxes, and their effects on
consumption. Lower purchases and higher taxes both push aggregate demand left. After the first-round
spending change, the multiplier process traces additional income-consumption rounds; crowding out can then
reduce the net effect. A tax cut raises disposable income, which can increase consumption and shift AD right.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Higher government purchases directly raise aggregate demand; lower purchases reduce it.
•
Tax changes affect aggregate demand indirectly by changing disposable income and consumption.
•
Expansionary fiscal policy shifts aggregate demand right; contractionary policy shifts it left.
•
Distinguish a shift of aggregate demand from a movement along the curve caused by the price level.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to classify fiscal policy from the economy's condition rather than the government's
action. Higher purchases or lower taxes are expansionary; lower purchases or higher taxes are
contractionary.
WORKED EXAMPLE: SHIFTING AGGREGATE DEMAND
At the displayed price level P, an expansionary fiscal action shifts
aggregate demand from AD1 to AD2. Real output demanded rises
from Y1 to Y2 at that fixed price level. A larger action could shift
demand to AD3 and quantity demanded to Y3, but the graph does
not establish which package is desirable; that depends on the
output gap and timing.
