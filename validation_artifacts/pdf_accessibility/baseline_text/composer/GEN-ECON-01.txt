Scarcity
THE CORE IDEA
Scarcity means people, businesses, and governments have limited resources but unlimited wants. Because
resources such as time, money, labor, and raw materials are limited, choices must be made. Every choice
involves a tradeoff, and those tradeoffs force individuals and societies to decide how to use scarce
resources. Scarcity is the basic problem that economics studies.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Resources are limited, but wants are greater than what can be produced.
•
A choice must be made between competing uses.
•
Getting more of one thing means giving up something else.
•
The question focuses on limits, tradeoffs, or unmet wants.
WATCH OUT
Scarcity does not mean there is almost none of a good. It means the good is limited relative to wants.
Even time and money are scarce because they have alternative uses.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CHOOSING BETWEEN SCARCE USES
A student has two hours before an exam and must choose between studying economics or finishing a
history paper. The student cannot fully do both. This is scarcity because time is limited, so choosing one
use means giving up the other.
