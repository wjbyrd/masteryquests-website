Limits of Monetary Control
THE CORE IDEA
The central bank influences rather than perfectly controls broad money because banks and the public choose
how much to lend, hold, deposit, or keep as cash. Excess reserve holding weakens the multiplier process. If
banks do not lend excess reserves, the deposit expansion process weakens. The Fed influences the money
supply, but banks and depositors affect reserves, lending, and deposits. When banks hold more excess
reserves, they lend less, so deposit creation weakens.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
The Fed influences the money supply, but banks and depositors affect reserves, lending, and deposits.
•
When banks hold more excess reserves, they lend less, so deposit creation weakens.
•
The Fed can influence money creation, but banks and the public also matter.
•
The central bank adds reserves, but banks choose to hold much of them as excess reserves.
•
Households convert part of each new bank deposit into currency rather than leaving the funds in banks.
WATCH OUT
Do not treat the central bank as having mechanical one-for-one control over broad money. The central
bank controls its own balance sheet and reserve conditions more directly than broad money, which also
depends on banks, borrowers, and the public.
WORKED EXAMPLE: WHY MONEY EXPANSION FALLS SHORT
The Fed adds $40 million of reserves, but banks hold half as excess reserves and the public keeps
some funds as cash. Because only part of the injection supports new loans and redeposits, broad
money rises by less than the simple multiplier predicts. The Fed controls reserves more directly than it
controls deposits held by the public.
