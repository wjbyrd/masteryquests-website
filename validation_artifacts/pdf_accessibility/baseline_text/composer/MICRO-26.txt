LRAC, Economies & Diseconomies of Scale
THE CORE IDEA
Long-run average cost shows the lowest attainable cost per unit when every input and plant size can vary.
Scale can make specialized tasks and equipment economical, lowering LRAC over a range. Diminishing
marginal product is a short-run concept; diseconomies concern scale when all inputs can vary. Falling
long-run average cost as output expands is economies of scale.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Diminishing marginal product is a short-run concept; diseconomies concern scale when all inputs can vary.
•
A learner reads a downward-sloping LRAC segment as diseconomies of scale.
•
The concepts apply to different horizons and input changes.
•
A very large firm becomes harder to coordinate.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse scale and diminishing returns. Diminishing marginal product is a short-run concept;
diseconomies concern scale when all inputs can vary.
WORKED EXAMPLE: READING LONG-RUN AVERAGE COST
The LRAC curve falls from about $20 at 30 units to its
minimum of about $12 at 60 units, showing
economies of scale. It then rises back toward $20 by
90 units, showing diseconomies of scale. The
cost-minimizing scale is approximately 60 units.
