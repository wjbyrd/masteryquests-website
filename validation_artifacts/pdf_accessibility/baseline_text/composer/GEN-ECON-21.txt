Tax Incidence without Elasticity
THE CORE IDEA
A tax creates a wedge between the price buyers pay and the price sellers receive. Legal assignment does
not determine who bears the economic burden. Equivalent taxes on buyers or sellers create the same wedge
and market outcome. Tax burden depends on market responses, not just who is legally assigned to pay.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Look for a per-unit tax that separates the price buyers pay from the price sellers receive.
•
Find the new quantity where the vertical gap between demand and supply equals the tax.
•
The buyer burden is the rise in the buyer price; the seller burden is the fall in the seller price.
•
Do not infer burden from who sends the tax payment to the government.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to assume that legal tax assignment determines the full economic burden. Tax
burden depends on market responses, not just who is legally assigned to pay.
WORKED EXAMPLE: TRACING A TAX WEDGE
Before the tax, buyers and sellers trade at $10.50.
After the tax is imposed on buyers, the graph shows
buyers paying $13.50 while sellers receive $7.50.
Each side is $3 away from the original price, so each
bears half of the $6 tax even though buyers are
legally responsible for remitting it.
