Price Signals
THE CORE IDEA
Prices coordinate decentralized decisions by communicating scarcity and changing the incentives facing
buyers and sellers. Information about waiting costs can redirect users much like a price signal. A higher price
raises the cost of consuming the good and encourages substitution or conservation. A higher price
communicates stronger scarcity or demand pressure and encourages supply responses. A supply
improvement can lower price by reducing the good’s relative scarcity.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A higher price communicates stronger scarcity or demand pressure and encourages supply responses.
•
The price of a product falls after a new production technology spreads.
•
A congested road has no toll.
•
A price rises while quantity sold also rises.
•
A shortage persists because a legal ceiling prevents price from rising.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to assume that a higher price always tells buyers and sellers to do the same thing.
It discourages quantity demanded while encouraging quantity supplied, coordinating opposite
responses.
WORKED EXAMPLE: USING A PRICE SIGNAL
A hospital posts current wait times of 90 minutes at Clinic A and 20 minutes at Clinic B. Patients who
can use either location shift toward Clinic B. The posted wait acts like a nonmoney price signal: it
communicates relative congestion and reallocates demand without changing the medical fee.
