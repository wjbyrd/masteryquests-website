Labor-Market Institutions
THE CORE IDEA
Labor-market institutions change wages, hiring, search incentives, and the balance between labor supplied
and labor demanded. Efficiency wages can improve effort and reduce turnover. A binding minimum wage can
create a surplus of labor, meaning more workers want jobs than firms want to hire. Unemployment insurance
can support job seekers, but may also reduce urgency to accept a job quickly.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A binding minimum wage can create a surplus of labor, meaning more workers want jobs than firms want to
hire.
•
Unemployment insurance can support job seekers, but may also reduce urgency to accept a job quickly.
•
A firm raises wages above equilibrium and worker effort rises enough to improve productivity, but fewer
workers are hired at the higher wage.
•
Unemployment insurance can reduce the cost of continued job search, which may lengthen unemployment
spells.
•
Union wage bargaining can raise wages above equilibrium and reduce the quantity of labor demanded.
WATCH OUT
Do not forget that a binding wage floor creates a surplus when labor supplied exceeds labor demanded.
A binding minimum wage can create a surplus of labor, meaning more workers want jobs than firms
want to hire.
WORKED EXAMPLE: BINDING MINIMUM WAGE
At a binding minimum wage, 120 workers want jobs and firms hire 90. The labor surplus is 120 - 90 = 30
workers. The wage is held above equilibrium, so the quantity of labor supplied exceeds the quantity
demanded.
