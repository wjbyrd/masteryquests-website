Determinants of Supply
THE CORE IDEA
Supply shifts when a nonprice determinant changes. Supply increases and shifts right when sellers can
provide more at every price; supply decreases and shifts left when they provide less at every price. Important
determinants include input and resource costs, technology and productivity, taxes and subsidies, expectations,
the number of sellers, and natural or regulatory production conditions. A change that lowers marginal
production cost generally increases supply, while a change that raises marginal cost generally decreases it.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Input prices, technology, productivity, taxes, or subsidies change.
•
Producers' expectations or the number of sellers changes.
•
Quantity supplied changes at the same price.
•
The entire supply curve moves left or right rather than moving to another point on one curve.
WATCH OUT
Do not treat a change in the good's own price as a supply shifter. Own-price changes create
movements along the existing curve. A change in production cost, technology, expectations, or the
number of sellers changes how much firms offer at every price and shifts the curve.
WORKED EXAMPLE: SUPPLY SHIFT
At a hamburger price of $9, the original curve S0 shows
quantity supplied of 200 thousand, while S1 shows 100
thousand. The product's price is unchanged, so the
100-thousand decrease is not a movement along S0. It is a
leftward shift from S0 to S1, consistent with a determinant
such as higher input costs reducing supply.
