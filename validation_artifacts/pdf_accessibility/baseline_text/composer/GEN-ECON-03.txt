Opportunity Cost
THE CORE IDEA
Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative given up when a choice is made. Scarcity makes
opportunity cost unavoidable because using time, money, or resources for one purpose prevents their use
elsewhere. The cost is the single best forgone option, valued from the decision maker's perspective, and it
can be monetary or nonmonetary.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Look for a choice between two or more alternatives under a time, money, or resource constraint.
•
Identify the next-best alternative that the decision maker gives up.
•
Value the forgone alternative from the decision maker's perspective.
•
Do not add every rejected option; opportunity cost is the single best alternative forgone.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to add the value of every forgone option. Opportunity cost is the value of the
single next-best alternative, not the sum of all alternatives.
WORKED EXAMPLE: CHOOSING THE NEXT-BEST OPTION
A student can spend Saturday working for $80, studying for an exam worth an estimated $60 benefit, or
attending a concert valued at $40. If the student studies, the best forgone alternative is the $80 work
shift. The opportunity cost of studying is therefore $80, not $120 and not the concert's $40 value.
