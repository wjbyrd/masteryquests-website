Market Structure & Product Differentiation
THE CORE IDEA
Monopolistic competition combines many firms and relatively easy entry with differentiated products and
limited price-setting power. Entry and exit operate in both models. Differentiation explains why an individual
firm faces downward-sloping demand despite many sellers and easy entry. The practice is differentiating the
service experience rather than relying on a legal barrier or identical product. Differentiation makes the firm’s
product an imperfect substitute, so its individual demand curve slopes downward.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A market has many sellers and easy entry.
•
A dental practice competes mainly through evening hours, online scheduling, and unusually fast
appointments.
•
A restaurant has many nearby rivals, but customers value its menu and atmosphere enough that a small
price increase does not drive everyone away.
•
A market has many small sellers and easy entry, but every seller offers exactly the same product.
•
A neighborhood café competes through atmosphere, menu, and location.
WATCH OUT
A common mistake is to misapply monopolistic competition rule. Differentiation explains why an
individual firm faces downward-sloping demand despite many sellers and easy entry.
WORKED EXAMPLE: IDENTIFYING PRODUCT DIFFERENTIATION
A city has many coffee shops, entry is relatively easy, and each shop offers a distinct menu and
atmosphere. Because customers view the shops as close but imperfect substitutes, each shop faces
downward-sloping demand and has limited price-setting power. Those facts identify monopolistic
competition.
