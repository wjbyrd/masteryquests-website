Costs of Inflation
THE CORE IDEA
Inflation redistributes purchasing power when it differs from expectations and creates shoeleather, menu, and
relative-price costs. Actual inflation was higher than expected, so borrowers repay with less valuable dollars.
Menu costs are costs of changing posted prices. Shoe-leather costs refer to the time and effort people spend
reducing money holdings when inflation is high. Shoeleather costs are the wasted effort people spend trying
to hold less money during inflation.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
Shoe-leather costs refer to the time and effort people spend reducing money holdings when inflation is
high.
•
Shoeleather costs are the wasted effort people spend trying to hold less money during inflation.
•
Menu costs are the costs firms face when they change posted prices.
•
Firms repeatedly changing posted prices incur menu costs.
•
Menu costs are the costs of changing posted prices.
WATCH OUT
Do not confuse shoeleather costs with menu costs. Shoeleather costs are the wasted effort people
spend trying to hold less money during inflation.
WORKED EXAMPLE: UNEXPECTED INFLATION REDISTRIBUTION
A lender makes a 9% loan expecting 3% inflation, so the expected real return is 6%. Actual inflation
turns out to be 10%, making the realized real return approximately 9% - 10% = -1%. Unexpected
inflation transfers purchasing power from the lender to the borrower.
