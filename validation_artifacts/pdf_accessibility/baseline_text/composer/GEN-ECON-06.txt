Positive vs. Normative Analysis
THE CORE IDEA
Positive analysis makes claims that evidence can test, while normative analysis applies value judgments
about what should be done. Identify what observation could support or contradict a testable claim. A positive
claim can be false. A normative claim ranks goals or outcomes using values; evidence can inform that
judgment but cannot settle the values alone.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
A transit study predicts that a lower fare will increase ridership.
•
Ridership data can test the prediction even if it turns out to be wrong.
•
A council member says parks should receive more funding because access is fair.
•
A commissioner says the city ought to favor safer streets even if travel takes longer.
WATCH OUT
Do not treat positive as good, true, or politically neutral. A positive claim is one that evidence could in
principle test, even if the claim is false.
WORKED EXAMPLE: POSITIVE OR NORMATIVE?
The statement 'A lower bus fare will increase ridership' is positive because ridership data can support or
contradict it. The statement 'The city should lower bus fares because access is fair' is normative
because it applies a value judgment. Evidence can inform both statements, but only the first is directly
testable as written.
