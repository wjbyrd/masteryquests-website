Economic Growth Policy
THE CORE IDEA
Long-run growth policy raises productive capacity rather than current spending alone. Saving and reliable
finance support capital formation; education builds human capital; research and competition can advance
technology. Stable property rights and institutions make firms more willing to invest. Because physical capital
faces diminishing returns, sustained growth usually depends on several of these channels working together.
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
•
The policy changes capital formation, human capital, technology, or institutions.
•
The question distinguishes productive capacity from a temporary demand increase.
•
Reliable finance and property rights affect whether saving becomes productive investment.
•
The proposed change can raise output per worker over time.
WATCH OUT
Do not treat a one-time demand boost as a substitute for policies that raise productive capacity.
Long-run growth policy works through productivity, capital formation, human capital, technology, and
institutions.
WORKED EXAMPLE: EVALUATING A GROWTH POLICY
Capital deepening from 20 to 40 units per worker raises output per
worker from 28.53 to 36.37, a gain of 7.84. Because the curve
flattens, capital accumulation alone faces diminishing returns.
Sustained growth also requires human capital, technology, and
institutions that raise productivity.
