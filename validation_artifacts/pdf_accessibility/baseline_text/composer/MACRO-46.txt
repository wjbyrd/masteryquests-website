THE CORE IDEA
In the course saving model, T is net tax revenue and G is government purchases. Public saving is T - G. A positive value is a budget
surplus; a negative value is a budget deficit. The deficit is G - T, the opposite sign. When fiscal data use total outlays instead, use the
categories stated in that dataset rather than silently mixing definitions.
?
HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT
-
Revenue above spending: surplus and positive public saving.
-
Spending above revenue: deficit and negative public saving.
-
Equal revenue and spending: balanced budget and zero public saving.
-
A deficit is a flow measured over a period; debt is an accumulated stock.
!
WATCH OUT
Keep signs straight: a $40 deficit means public saving is -$40, not +$40. State whether G means government purchases or the source's
broader outlays measure.

WORKED EXAMPLE: ONE BUDGET, TWO SIGN CONVENTIONS
KEY RELATIONSHIPS
Public saving = T - G
Surplus = T - G > 0
Deficit = G - T > 0
Balanced budget: T = G
Deficit is a flow; debt is a stock
Net tax revenue is $520 billion and government purchases are $560 billion.
Public saving is T - G = -$40 billion. The budget deficit is G - T = +$40
billion. Both statements describe the same imbalance using opposite signs.
