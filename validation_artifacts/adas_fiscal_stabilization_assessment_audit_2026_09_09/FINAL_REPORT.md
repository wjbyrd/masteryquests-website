## 9. True coverage gaps

No materially absent selected objective was found in the nine core concepts. No A gap, new question ID or new graph family was justified. Absent curricular subjects are boundaries, not reasons to expand the audit. Phillips content quality beyond AD-AS integration remains deferred.


## 10. Robustness gaps

Repetition is concentrated in upper-tier forms that identify a shift or perform one forward multiplication. These findings are chiefly F rather than absent-coverage B. The targeted revisions add inverse allocation, missing-evidence tests, policy timing and alternative stabilization targets. Similar introductory variants and some short upper-tier applications remain; this report does not claim uniform depth or empirical item discrimination.


## 11. Calibration gaps

Hard already includes sticky-wage applications, opposed determinants, capacity diagnosis, graph equilibrium chains, fiscal-versus-tax effects and stabilization dilemmas. 74 core canonical-Hard records retain their content; the full list is in `retained-hard.json`. Eleven canonical-Hard records receive targeted changes, including checkpoints that carry that difficulty. Ordinary-bank Hard remains at 105 for the complete preset.

The local C gap was graph-based multiplier integration. Revised ECON-SP-HARD-228 requires multiplier arithmetic, subtraction of an explicitly total AD offset, and mapping the net quantity onto the graph. It retains its ID, skill, type, tier and pool. No question was moved down from Legendary to manufacture Hard coverage.


## 12. Calculation and integration gaps

All 24 revised quantitative items were recomputed independently from final stems or original-image observations using rational arithmetic where applicable. `numerical-validation.json` includes the inputs, derivation, result and all four choices. Answer-hash verification was performed separately and was not used to establish the mathematics.

Core defects were mixing raw autonomous investment with total AD changes, equating horizontal AD distances with realized GDP gaps, and imposing exact arithmetic on hand-drawn curves. Revised tasks define model assumptions, distinguish direct/induced/offset effects and require needed evidence. No hidden equilibrium-price assumption is used to turn a horizontal demand shift into an equal GDP change.


## 13. AD-AS mechanics findings

The retained bank correctly distinguishes a price-level movement along AD/SRAS from determinant shifts, short-run supply from productive capacity, and current equilibrium output from potential output. The new upper AD items separate gross opposing determinants from a net observed shift and identify missing SRAS evidence. Labor-force-plus-energy questions now acknowledge opposing SRAS effects rather than silently assuming that the energy shock dominates.


## 14. Demand-shock findings

Negative demand shocks lower output and price in the standard upward-sloping-SRAS model; positive shocks raise both initially. Strong existing Hard questions already use the joint outcomes and adjustment chain. Fiscal offsets and graph-only demand diagrams now explicitly avoid claiming a complete equilibrium result without supply information.


## 15. Supply-shock findings

Adverse SRAS shifts raise the price level and lower output; favorable shifts reverse that pattern. ECON-SP-HARD-212 previously began on AS2 while claiming a displayed supply increase ending on AS2. It now starts at AD1-AS1 and compares both endpoints after rightward demand and supply shifts. The observed price cancellation is specific to that drawing, not a universal equal-shifts rule.
