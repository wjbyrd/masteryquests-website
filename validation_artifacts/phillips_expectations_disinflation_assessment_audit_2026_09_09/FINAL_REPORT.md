## 14. SRPC findings

With expectations, supply conditions and natural unemployment fixed, demand expansion moves up-left along SRPC; contraction reverses that movement. Supplied demand events are transferred into Phillips reasoning without reopening monetary mechanics. New Hard applications distinguish actual unemployment from the unchanged natural rate and use both axis changes.


## 15. LRPC findings

The vertical LRPC is correctly located at natural unemployment. Demand surprises do not permanently move it; labor-market matching changes can. The accelerationist correction distinguishes a constant required surprise from an ever-higher inflation rate under last-period adaptive expectations. Prompts predicting a return to natural now state when repeated efforts to maintain the below-natural gap stop.


## 16. Expectations findings

Actual inflation alone does not identify expected inflation. Two graph prompts now exclude supply/natural-rate shifts before attributing a curve shift to expectations. Hard 263/264 specify an initial equality of actual and expected inflation before concluding that unemployment crosses the natural rate; Hard 246 explicitly excludes a supply offset.

PG5-PC-L-006 uses B=(6,6), a stated Phillips equation and a measured +1.1 supply offset to infer expected inflation of 5.5. Without that independent measurement, only expected inflation plus the supply offset is identified. The graph does not supply a unique causal diagnosis on its own.


## 17. Adaptive and rational expectations

Adaptive tasks use observed inflation or forecast errors; the new numerical graph continuations explicitly specify one-period updating rather than treating it as a universal adjustment speed. They compare holding unemployment fixed with holding inflation fixed, or impose a later no-deflation constraint.

The revised Hard credibility item states that rational expectations use available information, including credible policy announcements. Forecast errors can occur with new information, and nominal contracts can still delay adjustment. Neither rational expectations nor an announcement alone guarantees perfect forecasting or zero output cost.


## 18. Disinflation findings

The existing Hard b-to-d-to-c sequence already distinguishes movement along SRPC2 from a subsequent downward SRPC shift. The revised Legendary comparison contrasts contracts that retain old expectations with immediate expectations/contract adjustment, making the different transition paths conditional. Another upper item requires a forecast of natural-rate return at fixed inflation to be reconciled with an appropriate lower SRPC. Disinflation remains slower price growth, not necessarily falling prices.


## 19. Sacrifice-ratio findings

The course convention is cumulative output loss relative to potential, expressed as a percentage of an annual output benchmark or a sum of annual percentage shortfalls, divided by the percentage-point inflation reduction. Dollar-loss-per-point tasks remain explicitly dollar-valued, not dimensionless ratios. Different denominators and annual benchmarks are not mixed.

New upper tasks reconcile omitted earlier losses, compare total versus per-point cost, solve a remaining-stage loss ceiling, and infer stage-specific ratios from the combined ratio. Unsupported judgments such as “not extreme” are removed in favor of explicit comparisons. No valid legacy numerical key was reversed solely to increase revisions.


## 20. AD-AS to Phillips integration

Demand-driven output gains map to lower cyclical unemployment and inflation pressure along a fixed SRPC; adverse supply can raise both inflation and unemployment and shift the relationship. The supply-shock feedback correction now describes an initial adverse shift followed by expectations reinforcement, not the disappearance of a demand-created unemployment gain. All underlying AD-AS and fiscal definitions remain unchanged.


## 21. Supply-shock findings

The bank already distinguishes stagflation from movement along a fixed downward SRPC. An upper revision separates a temporary supply offset that disappears from higher expectations that persist after contracts reset. The final curve is above the original, while its position relative to the disruption-period curve needs the relative magnitudes; no unique explanation is inferred from inflation alone.


## 22. Natural-rate findings

The revised matching-reform item separates a 0.8-point structural natural-rate decline from a 0.7-point cyclical gap below the new rate. Long-run unemployment returns to 5.6 percent after the demand surprise ends. It uses an original matching-reform task, not the exam’s demographic scenario. Cyclical recession is not treated as an automatic permanent LRPC shift.
