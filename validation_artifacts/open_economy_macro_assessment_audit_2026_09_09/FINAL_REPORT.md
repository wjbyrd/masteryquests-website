## 14. Exchange-rate conventions

Nominal questions may use either explicitly named reciprocal quote. Graphs and course e use **foreign currency per domestic currency**, with the U.S. dollar domestic in FX images. A rise in this quote is domestic appreciation; a rise in its reciprocal is depreciation. Thirteen record-level quote/unit clarifications are enumerated in `defect-classification.json`; none silently converts the course formula.

Reviews MACRO-53/54 and all ten image axes support this convention. FX-01–08 use upward-sloping gross supply; FX-09/10 use vertical net supply. Both models are retained and named where their differing quantity implications matter.


## 15. Nominal and real rates

Course formula: **ε = eP/P***. Literal physical-basket questions now specify currency-denominated comparable basket prices. Unit cancellation yields foreign baskets per domestic basket. Price-index ratios are normalized real indices, not automatically physical basket counts. Nominal appreciation implies real appreciation only with suitable relative-price assumptions.

RER-M-004 incorrectly claimed that nominal depreciation and slower domestic inflation offset. Both lower eP/P*, so the corrected answer is real depreciation. RER-L-003, LB-001 and LB-003 now distinguish exact compensating changes from first-order inflation approximations. RER-R-001 and EL-001 had overlapping valid alternatives; both are corrected.


## 16. NX and NCO

NX = X − M; NCO = resident foreign-asset acquisition − foreign domestic-asset acquisition. Negative NCO is net inflow. NX = NCO is period accounting, not an immediate causal claim about one bond purchase; TX-H-006 now makes that distinction. NCO-B3-003 distinguishes period net acquisitions including sales from changes in asset stocks. No remaining reversed NX/NCO sign was found.


## 17. Open-economy saving identity

S = I + NCO = I + NX is used consistently. New inverse tasks reconcile public/private saving, domestic real investment and foreign finance rather than treating financial asset purchases as domestic capital formation. No closed-economy S = I substitution remains in keyed content.


## 18. Loanable-funds extension

The actual selected POL-H-003 states national-saving supply and I+NCO demand with the domestic real interest rate equilibrating. This establishes the open extension without imposing it on the gross FX graphs. Prior closed-economy saving and loanable-funds modules remain identical.


## 19. NCO and interest rates

Higher domestic real returns reduce NCO, other things equal. Foreign returns and domestic political risk can independently shift desired NCO. NCO-H-002 retains ambiguity; NCO-L-002 now separates an observed net increase from the negative partial interest-rate effect. NCO-LB-002 reconstructs schedule shift and rate feedback from gross-flow evidence.


## 20. FX-market findings

Gross-market graphs measure quantity of dollars exchanged, not automatically NCO or NX. FX-LB-001 now requires separate asset-flow evidence before inferring NCO. In the policy graphs, supply is vertical and equals NCO; demand equals NX. FX-R-001 no longer incorrectly denies movement along vertical supply: changing the rate along that curve leaves quantity fixed, while changing NCO shifts it.

Simultaneous-shift items name the upward-sloping model where quantity is ambiguous. Horizontal shift magnitudes are compared at the same original exchange rate. FX-LB-002 and POL-LB-002 identify direction while recognizing missing slope information for exact rate changes.


## 21. PPP findings

PPP remains a long-run arbitrage benchmark with transport costs, trade barriers, nontraded services and basket differences. Relative PPP uses explicit quote direction and distinguishes exact growth factors from first-order inflation differences. Upper items now diagnose the horizon and source of deviations; none treats PPP as an exact daily law.


## 22. Fiscal policy in the open economy

With private saving and other determinants appropriately held fixed: deficit → S falls → real interest rate rises → I and NCO fall → vertical FX supply falls → real appreciation → NX falls. Hard already carries this chain. POL-LB-001 now recovers the private-saving offset from observed investment and NX changes; protected fiscal/ADAS questions are untouched.


## 23. Trade policy

In the selected long-run model with unchanged S and I, a tariff raises NX demand at a given real exchange rate but appreciation restores NX to NCO. POL-H-002 now explicitly says **real exchange rate**, avoiding confusion with the interest rate. POL-L-003 reconstructs the required import rebound and detects an inconsistent measured offset. Tariff effects are not treated as a guaranteed permanent trade-balance gain.


## 24. Capital flight

The risk shock shifts desired NCO outward, increases loanable-funds demand and raises the domestic real rate. Domestic investment falls; rate feedback restrains but does not necessarily reverse the NCO increase. Greater final NCO shifts vertical FX supply right and causes depreciation with NX demand fixed. POL-L-002 and NCO-LB-002 independently reconstruct the shift-versus-feedback components.


## 25. Exchange rate → NX → AD
