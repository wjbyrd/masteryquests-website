## Why six instead of simply using OLI.1–OLI.6

The inherited objectives are close, but OLI.4 and OLI.5 mix two pedagogically different kinds of strategic behavior.

The audit therefore recombines their records by actual primary skill:

### Dynamic Strategy: Repeated Games, Credibility & Entry Deterrence

Includes:

- repeated games
- credible threats
- credible commitments
- entry deterrence
- limit pricing

This is the natural home for the bank's higher-end dynamic game theory. It is intentionally classified **supporting/advanced**, not a required standalone core topic.

### Tacit Coordination, Price Leadership & Nonprice Competition

Includes:

- tacit coordination
- price leadership
- price wars
- explicit-collusion contrasts
- nonprice competition
- kinked-demand material

This is the more conventional Principles treatment of oligopoly rivalry outside formal matrix analysis.

This split gives faculty a meaningful choice that the original OLI.4/OLI.5 boundary does not.

## Game-theory scope finding

The user's concern about game theory is supported by the bank.

The **Game Theory Foundations** child is normal Principles material: payoff order, players/strategies/payoffs, best responses, dominant strategies, pure-strategy Nash equilibrium, and cooperative versus noncooperative outcomes.

The **Dynamic Strategy** material goes materially further. The current bank contains at least:

- **31 records** using infinite-horizon / continuation-factor / present-value repeated-game reasoning.
- **28 records** using backward induction, sequential entry games, or sequential-rationality reasoning.

These questions are not mathematically or economically defective. They were part of the approved Phase 6.2i publisher-scale design. But they are not universal Principles coverage. They should therefore be **isolated rather than deleted**.

Recommended composer treatment:

- Keep `oligopoly` as the complete parent family for instructors who want the full bank.
- Make **Game Theory Foundations** a normal standalone selector.
- Make **Collusion, Cartels & Prisoner’s-Dilemma Incentives** a normal standalone selector.
- Mark **Dynamic Strategy: Repeated Games, Credibility & Entry Deterrence** as supporting/advanced.
- Do not include Dynamic Strategy in any future "Oligopoly Core" quick-start unless explicitly selected.

This resolves the pedagogical concern without amputating valid material.

## Recommended authoring boundaries

The 75-question backfill should follow these rules:

1. **No formal Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg, mixed strategies, Bayesian games, or advanced repeated-game proofs.**
2. New Game Theory Foundations questions should stay at normal Principles matrix-reading and pure-strategy reasoning.
3. Do not add more repeated-game PV calculations or backward-induction trees; the bank already has ample advanced material.
4. New Dynamic Strategy Easy questions should be conceptual: why future interaction can matter, what makes a threat credible, and why commitment can change entry incentives.
5. Kinked demand remains supplemental inside Rivalry & Coordination; do not make it the dominant content of the child.
6. Antitrust questions must avoid time-sensitive legal thresholds and detailed case law.
7. No new graph assets unless a specific authored question cannot be made unambiguous without one.
8. Preserve the existing 370 question records semantically except for taxonomy metadata.

## Final recommendation

Lock the six proposed subtopics and authorize a **75-question targeted adaptive retrofit**.

The key pedagogical upgrade is not the question count. It is the isolation of higher-end dynamic game theory. The resulting composer can support:

- a conventional Principles Oligopoly review using structure, basic game theory, collusion/cartels, rivalry, and welfare/policy;
- a narrower matrix/Nash quiz;
- a cartel/prisoner's-dilemma quiz;
- or an explicitly advanced repeated-games/entry-deterrence build for instructors who actually teach that material.

That is the granularity this family was missing.
