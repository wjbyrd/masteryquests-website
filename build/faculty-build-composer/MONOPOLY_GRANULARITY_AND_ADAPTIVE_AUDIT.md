## Why seven

### Monopoly power and barriers stay together

`MON.1` is the conceptual entrance to monopoly: market power, legal barriers, control of key resources, network effects, and natural-monopoly sources. It is a coherent instructor-facing selector, but it is intentionally classified as **supporting** because a full 30-room campaign built only around definitions and barrier types would be forced and repetitive.

It pairs naturally with **Monopoly Demand, Revenue & Marginal Revenue** for broader introductory monopoly practice.

### Demand/revenue and output/price remain separate

`MON.2` and `MON.3` should not be collapsed. Students can understand why a monopolist faces downward-sloping demand and why MR lies below price while still failing the separate decision sequence:

1. choose the quantity where MR and MC support profit maximization; then
2. move to the demand curve to identify the monopoly price.

That is a real conceptual distinction and deserves separate selectors.

### Profit, loss, and shutdown stay together

`MON.4` is large, but the content forms one coherent firm-outcome block: calculate profit or loss, distinguish persistent monopoly profit from competitive long-run adjustment, and determine whether a loss-making monopolist should continue operating or shut down.

Unlike Perfect Competition, splitting shutdown away here would create an eighth selector without enough pedagogical payoff. The monopoly shutdown rule is best interpreted alongside monopoly loss and cost conditions.

### Welfare and competitive comparison form one block

`MON.5` naturally combines competitive-versus-monopoly outcomes, consumer surplus, allocative inefficiency, deadweight loss, and rent seeking. These are all consequences of monopoly market power rather than separate production decisions.

### `MON.6` must be split

The inherited `MON.6` objective is too broad. It contains two different teaching units:

- **Natural Monopoly & Regulation**: marginal-cost pricing, average-cost pricing, price caps, regulatory tradeoffs, and natural-monopoly cost structure.
- **Price Discrimination**: conditions for discrimination, resale prevention, first-degree and third-degree discrimination, and welfare consequences.

Keeping those under one granular selector would repeat the exact problem this project is fixing.
