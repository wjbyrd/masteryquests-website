Before a tax, price is $20 and quantity is 1,000. Afterwards buyers pay $24, sellers receive $18 and 850 units trade. An analyst says the buyer burden is the entire tax and calculates revenue using the original quantity. Which correction is right?
A:The wedge is $6, buyers bear $4 per unit and sellers $2; revenue is $5,100. [KEY]
B:The wedge is $4, buyers bear $4 per unit and sellers $0; revenue is $3,400.
C:The wedge is $6, buyers bear $4 per unit and sellers $2; revenue is $6,000.
D:The wedge is $6, buyers bear $6 per unit and sellers $0; revenue is $5,100.
Feedback: The wedge is $24−$18=$6, split into the $4 buyer-price increase and $2 seller-receipt decrease. Only 850 units are taxed, so revenue is $6×850=$5,100.

A tax produces a $6 wedge and 850 sales, down from 1,000 without tax. A proposed increase would produce an $8 wedge and 600 sales. Which comparison correctly evaluates revenue and lost trades?
A:Revenue falls from $5,100 to $4,800 while lost trades increase from 150 to 400. [KEY]
B:Revenue rises from $5,100 to $6,800 while lost trades remain 150.
C:Revenue falls from $6,000 to $4,800 while lost trades decrease from 400 to 150.
D:Revenue rises from $5,100 to $6,000 while lost trades increase from 150 to 400.
Feedback: Revenue uses the quantity remaining under each tax: $6×850=$5,100 versus $8×600=$4,800. Relative to 1,000 untaxed trades, losses are 150 and 400. A higher rate does not guarantee more revenue.

An unregulated market trades 100 units. A binding ceiling makes sellers offer 60 units while buyers want 140; a tax instead produces 70 trades with buyers paying $12 and sellers receiving $9. Assume no evasion or government purchases. Which policy comparison follows?
A:The ceiling limits trades to 60 and leaves a shortage of 80; the tax permits 70 trades and raises $210. [KEY]
B:The ceiling permits 140 trades and leaves a shortage of 80; the tax permits 70 trades and raises $210.
C:The ceiling limits trades to 60 and leaves a shortage of 40; the tax permits 70 trades and raises $300.
D:The ceiling limits trades to 100 and leaves a shortage of 40; the tax permits 70 trades and raises $210.
Feedback: Ceiling trades are limited by the 60 units supplied, and shortage is 140−60=80. Under the tax, the $3 wedge on 70 actual trades yields $210. The two policies reduce trades by different mechanisms.

Untaxed price is $30. A $7 tax makes buyers pay $34 and sellers receive $27. Lawmakers propose collecting the same tax from buyers instead of sellers in the same competitive market, with unchanged demand, supply and enforcement. Which prediction correctly combines the observed burdens with the proposed change?
A:Buyers bear $4 and sellers $3 per unit; collecting from buyers shifts the full $7 burden to buyers.
B:Buyers bear $4 and sellers $3 per unit; changing the statutory payer alone does not change those economic burdens. [KEY]
C:Buyers bear $3 and sellers $4 per unit; changing the statutory payer alone does not change those burdens.
D:Buyers bear $7 and sellers $0 per unit; collecting from sellers would reverse those burdens.
Feedback: The buyer-price increase is $4 and seller-receipt decrease is $3, summing to $7. Moving collection between sides does not change the equilibrium wedge or relative responsiveness in the stated competitive setting.

In a competitive market, a per-unit tax initially raises buyer price, lowers seller receipts and reduces trades. Several years later suppliers can adjust output much more easily, while demand responsiveness is unchanged. Which statement correctly distinguishes the initial effect from the later burden comparison?
A:Initially buyers bear the full wedge; as supply becomes more elastic, sellers necessarily bear the full wedge.
B:Initially both sides bear part of the wedge; more elastic supply tends to move a larger share onto sellers.
C:Initially both sides bear part of the wedge; as supply becomes more elastic, a larger share tends to fall on buyers. [KEY]
D:Initially seller receipts rise; later statutory collection determines which side bears the wedge.
Feedback: The initial wedge separates a higher buyer price from a lower seller receipt. In the later comparison, suppliers have better adjustment options, so the relatively less responsive demand side tends to bear more of the tax.
