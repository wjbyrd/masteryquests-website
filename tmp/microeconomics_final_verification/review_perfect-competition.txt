A:No; revenue must exceed $100,000 to cover those costs.
B:No; implicit costs are excluded from opportunity cost.
C:Yes; explicit costs alone equal revenue.
D:Yes; revenue equals the full $100,000 economic cost. [KEY]
Feedback: 75,000+25,000=100,000. Zero economic profit includes payment for the owner’s forgone alternatives.

break_even
A firm chooses Q=40 at P=$15 and ATC=$15. What are total revenue, total cost and economic profit?
A:TR=$600; TC=$15; profit=$585
B:TR=$15; TC=$600; profit=−$585
C:TR=$600; TC=$600; profit=$0 [KEY]

Feedback: Both TR and TC equal 15×40=$600, so the firm breaks even economically.

Incumbents earn positive economic profit. Entry is easy, and new firms arrive while market demand stays fixed. What initial market adjustment tends to erode the profit?
A:Demand contracts because entrants buy the incumbents’ goods.
B:Supply expands and market price falls. [KEY]
C:Supply contracts and market price rises.
D:Each incumbent’s fixed cost necessarily disappears.
Feedback: New sellers add output at each price. With unchanged demand, the supply expansion lowers price and incumbent profit.

normal_profit
An owner requires $30,000 to match the best alternative use of its resources. The business earns exactly that amount after explicit costs. What are normal profit and economic profit?
A:Normal profit=$0; economic profit=$30,000
B:Both equal $30,000
C:Both equal $0
D:Normal profit=$30,000; economic profit=$0 [KEY]
Feedback: The $30,000 covers implicit opportunity cost. Economic profit is the amount above that required return, here zero.
