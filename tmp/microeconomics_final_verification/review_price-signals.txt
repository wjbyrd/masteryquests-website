price_quantity_inference
Price and quantity both rise. A report concludes that demand increased and that supply could not have changed. Which interpretation is strongest?
A:Only a supply increase fits because more output always means greater supply.
B:A demand increase alone fits the observations, but simultaneous supply changes are possible; the two observations do not identify every shift. [KEY]
C:Only a supply decrease fits because a higher price always means reduced supply.
D:Demand and supply must both be unchanged because equilibrium quantities cannot rise together with price.
Feedback: A rightward demand shift raises both price and quantity with supply fixed. But combined shifts can produce the same observed directions, so the evidence supports a possible explanation rather than uniquely identifying every determinant.

loss_signal_allocation
Resources currently used in a product would generate next year’s revenue of $120 and total opportunity cost of $140. An alternative use would generate revenue of $90 and opportunity cost of $70. Past spending is unrecoverable. Which allocation follows from the expected net-value signals?
A:Stay because $120 revenue exceeds the alternative’s $90, even though opportunity cost is higher.
B:Stay until past spending is recovered because sunk costs determine the best future use.
C:Move to the alternative: expected net value improves from −$20 to +$20, regardless of unrecoverable past spending. [KEY]
D:Move only if the alternative’s revenue exceeds $140, ignoring its lower opportunity cost.
Feedback: The current use has net value 120−140=−20; the alternative has 90−70=20. The $40 improvement concerns future alternatives. Unrecoverable past spending does not change that comparison.

corrected_price_signal
A trip gives a driver benefit $6, uses $3 of private resources and imposes $4 of delay on others. A congestion toll equals the external delay cost and its collection uses no resources. Which prediction explains the corrected price signal?
A:The driver takes the trip because the $4 toll is a transfer and therefore cannot affect private incentives.
B:The driver declines because the toll creates $4 of additional social resource cost beyond the original delay.
C:The driver takes the trip because only the $3 private resource use can enter the efficiency comparison.
D:The driver faces $7 in private cost plus toll and declines the trip; this avoids a trip whose $7 social resource cost exceeds its $6 benefit. [KEY]
Feedback: Without the toll the driver sees benefit 6 versus private cost 3. The $4 toll makes the private comparison reflect the $7 social cost. The payment is a transfer in welfare accounting, but it changes the driver’s incentive.
