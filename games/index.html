Engine transparency

“Adaptive” means different things in different modes.

Mastery Quests does not hide one black-box recommendation behind every activity. The engine changes how it selects questions, whether it can trigger remediation, and how strongly it interprets the result according to the mode the learner is actually playing.

Full-evidence modes

Adapt, remediate, confirm

Standard Campaign, Timed Trial, Exam Drill, Score Attack, and Unlimited Practice use the full adaptive selector. The engine tracks correctness, response time, concept, objective, skill, question type, difficulty exposure, and recent performance. Meaningful weakness can trigger targeted Repair, Bridge, and Retest work.

Challenge mode

Legendary adapts without detours

Legendary Mode uses adaptive challenge selection but does not stop for remediation. Its purpose is to test whether strong performance survives harder, less forgiving questions.

Short-run modes

Sample first. Investigate second.

Quiz uses balanced sampling and deliberately does not chase weakness. Fading Fortune, Risk & Reward, and Trial by Graph use a cold-start calibration period followed by constrained adaptive investigation. Their fixed-length reports distinguish weak signals from confirmed evidence.

Evidence-aware reporting

A short run does not pretend to be a full diagnosis.

In limited-run modes, one or two observations are a Limited Signal, three or four are an Emerging Pattern, and five or more form a Meaningful Sample. A sampled area needs at least five observations before the report can treat it as a confirmed weakness.
