## Findings and changes by concept

Ratings below describe the original questions semantically, not merely their stored type labels. “Direct” means the operation or required inputs are supplied; “embedded” means the student must identify them. All objectives had scenarios, but many were a recognizable label embedded in a short business story. No original question contained an actual missing-entry cost table. The second table records exact original type counts for every objective.

## Difficulty recalibration

- **Easy:** 88/90 unchanged; two recognition items revised for originality or clearer context.
- **Medium:** 86/90 unchanged; four revisions provide short numerical controls and different incentive/hidden-cost scenarios.
- **Hard:** 61/90 revised. Ordinary exam-style input selection, category identification, implicit-cost accounting, numerical policy interpretation and embedded marginal decisions are now common. Retained examples preserve useful variety instead of replacing every item.
- **Elite:** 60/90 revised. Work combines categories, reconstructs missing totals, interprets schedules, distinguishes marginal from average profitability and diagnoses partially correct managerial fixes. Some information is deliberately irrelevant but explicitly identified as unchanged.
- **Legendary:** 64/90 revised. New work includes reverse thresholds, expected values, nonmonotone schedules, indivisible job packages, optimal effort versus preferred contract, repeated learning and multiyear scope decisions. Greater difficulty comes from linked reasoning rather than large numbers.
- **Legendary Boss:** 31/90 revised, spanning all 27 teaching objectives plus integration. These include distinct synthesis scenarios and an explicit correction to the ambiguous existing tax boundary. The 59 retained boss items provide shorter checks; this remains the largest source of uneven upper-tier demand.
- Existing Easy Boss, Medium Boss and Final Boss pools remain unchanged. They retain checkpoint IDs, objective coverage and their existing supporting roles.

## Originality and sequence

Exam content was read as an assessment blueprint. New settings and decision structures include staged supplier capacity, reverse revenue reconstruction, complete feasible job combinations, verified resolution metrics, expected repair recovery and multiyear acquisition limits. The changes are not renamed or renumbered copies of exam questions. Existing closely similar hospitality/hidden-cost contexts were replaced. The exam is neither copied into the repository nor delivered as an answer bank.

No demand/supply graphs were added for ceilings, floors, taxes, transactions or surplus. Those questions use stated quantities, reservation values and prices. Tax receipts are distinguished from destroyed gains; binding controls do not automatically imply an illegal market or an exact welfare loss. Trial by Graph retains zero candidates and remains disabled. Learning questions explicitly define the progress ratio rather than inheriting ambiguous terminology.

## Remaining concerns and maintenance

Difficulty is an editorial calibration, not an empirical psychometric result. Actual exposure still depends on the preserved adaptive selector. Legendary Boss retains 59 shorter records and the main Legendary pool retains 26; these are the clearest remaining sources of uneven demand. LO7.3 has only one Hard record, now revised; most other Hard skill pools have two or more. Numerical price-floor and tax subskills are intentionally modest pools under LO2.2. Its existing four repair/four bridge questions cover barriers generally, rather than each newly represented numerical subskill separately. No pool was inflated to conceal these limitations.

Because question types and common-error identifiers were preserved exactly for compatibility, those historical labels are not a fresh cognitive-demand taxonomy. Actual stems and feedback carry the revised reasoning. Some original generic hints or timing expectations may fit the richer content less precisely; no UI or engine changes were made.

The private faculty source and original publisher were not found in the canonical repository. The published bank is updated, and [apply_content_patch.py](apply_content_patch.py) replays [content-patch.json](content-patch.json) after future publishing. It checks original-record fingerprints, accepts already-applied records, and stops on upstream conflicts before writing. Merging the patch into the private faculty source remains an upstream maintenance action when that source is available.

Exam inspection used complete Word text and all three table structures; the document contains no inline images. The packaged renderer could not run because bundled LibreOffice was unavailable, so no page-layout claims are made about the source exam. No exam content is reproduced in these audit artifacts.

No gameplay, UI, telemetry, access control, other games or course order were changed. No deployment, commit or push was performed.
