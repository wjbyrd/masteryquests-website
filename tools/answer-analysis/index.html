    Data support and methodology

    What these results mean

    Accuracy uses accepted, graded responses with usable saved question details. Repeated practice counts as responses, never as separate students. Runs may belong to the same student. Unique students are not available.

    Imports and filters

    CSV files use the original text parser. Excel (.xlsx) and modern, unencrypted Apple Numbers (.numbers) files are read locally. Each Numbers table is inspected separately. All tables with recognizable telemetry headers are imported as one file; non-telemetry sheets are listed as skipped. Empty cells and text identifiers are preserved. Formula, date-converted, and unsafe numeric cells are rejected with an explanation. Spreadsheet changes made before submission cannot be reversed. Spreadsheet parser license and attribution.

    Matching attempt identifiers count once across overlapping files. Answers without reliable identifiers are retained with a warning. Rapid guesses and unfinished exam selections are excluded from accuracy. Revised questions remain separate. Files without usable snapshots cannot supply question analysis. Reloading clears the report.

    Response filters apply to accuracy, timing, difficulty, coverage, encounters, and remediation responses. Run metrics describe the whole runs represented by those selected answers, including responses outside the filters. Runs containing no selected usable answers are not represented.

    Timing and endings

    Median response time is the primary timing measure. The mean retains all positive recorded times, including long pauses. Missing, invalid, negative, or zero response times are excluded because older exporters use zero as an unmeasured default. Short and long times do not establish guessing, confusion, or attention.

    Successful completion means an explicit normal mode-completion event, not mastery or a passing score. Early endings, recorded busts, conflicting outcomes, and unknown endings are separate. Successful completion rate is observed successful runs divided by all represented runs; unknown runs remain in the denominator. If no outcome can be identified, the rate is unavailable.

    Successful completion time uses successful ending clocks only. Answer counts per success require an exported end-of-run attempt count. Median overall run duration uses each run’s highest recorded clock and may understate incomplete exports. Mean ended-run duration includes early and unsuccessful endings; it is not successful completion time.

    Difficulty, encounters, and support

    Difficulty uses the recorded difficulty field in both local and private CSV exports. Missing labels and conflicting labels for the same saved question version are Unknown. The analysis does not fill missing difficulty from the mode field. Current banks are never downloaded to reconstruct historical content.

    The original exposure filter counts earlier stored answers and rapid guesses for a question ID within a run. Exact-version encounters additionally use saved revisions and reliable sequence numbers. A prior count of zero identifies a first retained encounter; a repeat requires an earlier matching revision. Incomplete retained history and ambiguous ordering may leave encounters unavailable. Skill encounters are unavailable because the inspected exports lack a consistent skill identifier and encounter history.

    Repair, bridge, and retest roles use explicit stage metadata. Linked post-remediation retests additionally require the same run, origin attempt, an observed incorrect origin, and a reliably ordered repair or bridge before the retest. This does not demonstrate a causal learning gain. Linked groups do not establish sequence initiation, completion, or abandonment. Checkpoint retry rates remain unavailable without explicit retry semantics.

    Coverage and review

    Coverage counts exact saved question versions and the number reaching five or ten graded responses. Bank percentage is unavailable without an authoritative inventory. Topic and objective tables show coverage depth for their own responses. The review minimum determines suggestions, not which responses enter totals. Fewer than five graded responses or two runs is limited evidence. A common incorrect choice is an observed answer pattern, not a proven misconception or a confirmed question-design problem.
