What counts as evidence here?

Modes and student control

Faculty set the boundaries. Students choose only the decisions that belong to the mode.

Mode selection

This build enables all seven modes so the full menu is visible. In a faculty-generated quest, modes that were not enabled in the composer are removed from this screen rather than merely disabled.

Question variety

The same quest can move beyond ordinary multiple-choice recall into graphs, calculations, and applied reasoning.

Conceptual practice

The engine mixes direct conceptual questions with interpretation and application. Difficulty can rise or fall from the same faculty-selected concept pool rather than locking students into a single fixed sequence.

Adaptive support and checkpoints

The engine does more than mark answers right or wrong. It can route support, reconnect the idea, and test mastery at checkpoints.

Targeted Repair

When performance exposes a specific weak skill, the engine can route to a focused Repair question instead of simply serving another random item. Repair is meant to rebuild the missing piece.

Open full screenshot →

Bridge back to the idea

After Repair, Bridge questions reconnect the repaired skill to the original reasoning. That keeps remediation from becoming an isolated side exercise that never returns to the main concept.

Open full screenshot →

Checkpoint scaffold

Standard and Legendary campaigns use checkpoint encounters as mastery checks. The checkpoint system builds a short scaffold from an eligible learning objective rather than pulling three unrelated hard questions.

Inside the quest

Challenge, pacing, and progression

Different modes change the pressure without pretending that speed, campaign progress, and mastery are the same thing.

Legendary Mode

Legendary Mode raises the pressure with mastery-level, multi-step, and closer-choice questions while retaining the campaign checkpoint structure. It is a challenge mode, not just a cosmetic difficulty label.

Open full screenshot →

Timed Trial

Timed Trial runs against a visible ten-minute clock. Correct answers advance, misses can create setbacks, and the run is tracked separately from Standard Campaign progress.

Open full screenshot →

Score Attack

Score Attack turns accuracy, streaks, and speed into an arcade-style score. The visible score and best-score HUD make fluency pressure obvious without confusing it with the mastery evidence itself.

Open full screenshot →

Milestones and progression

Inside the quest

Mastery Report 2.0

Accuracy answers “how did the student perform?” Evidence strength answers “how much weight should we put on that result?”

Expert profile — Mastery Demonstrated

This controlled student profile finished at 92% with repeated advanced and high-challenge exposure. The report awards Mastery Demonstrated because the performance is both strong and supported by enough difficult evidence.

Open full screenshot →

Average profile — Strong Evidence

This student performs reasonably well but still exposes targeted weaknesses. Strong Evidence means the diagnosis has enough weight to trust; it does not mean the student has mastered the material.

Open full screenshot →

Weak profile — Strong Evidence of weakness

The weak profile is the important stress test. A large run can produce Strong Evidence while the performance itself remains poor—exactly the distinction needed to prevent a confident diagnosis from being mistaken for mastery.
