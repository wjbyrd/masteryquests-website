Choose how students practice

Ten ways to play.

Faculty enable the modes that fit the assignment. After selection, the chosen Guide introduces the Quest; the Game Menu can replay that introduction, manage fullscreen, and show the rotating Daily Task as it progresses alongside normal play.

One menu, ten practice goals

The generated game displays only the modes enabled by the instructor. This demonstration build enables all ten. Availability in another build or collection depends on its content and settings.

Open full screenshot →

Standard Campaign

Full 30-room mastery progression with milestones, setbacks, saved progress, and checkpoints.

Timed Trial

A ten-minute run where correct answers advance and misses push the student back.

Exam Drill

Nine-question sections support answered/unanswered navigation, review, and revision before deliberate checkpoint commitment. Correctness stays hidden while the section is active, and artifact powers are disabled.

Quiz

A fixed 1-15 question run using the concepts already selected by the instructor.

Unlimited Practice

Adaptive practice that continues until the student ends the session and generates a report.

Legendary Mode

A full campaign built from mastery-level questions with checkpoints and no soft landing.

Score Attack

Accuracy, streaks, and speed build the score; misses reduce it.

Trial by Graph

A fixed run made only from audited questions where the graph is required.

Fading Fortune

Wrong choices disappear over time while the available value falls from 100 toward 25 points.

Risk & Reward

Students risk 10%, 25%, 50%, or all of their bankroll before the question appears.

Diagnose mastery

Mastery Report 2.0 turns a run into a next step.

The report shows how the student performed and how much information the game has to judge that performance. It can suggest a Concept Review for an area that needs work. In this example, the student struggled with scarcity and questions that asked them to apply a concept.

From Student Results to Class-Level Feedback

Collect Mastery Reports and game-data CSVs through your LMS, then open Class Answer Analysis to combine the files. Compare topics and learning objectives, check the response counts, and open questions to see the choices students selected.

Synthetic demonstration: these screenshots use six invented game runs with 48 accepted responses and 20 correct answers (42%). They illustrate the functioning tool and contain no student records.

Copy the class summary, review its sample-size qualifications, and adapt the wording for your course. The response patterns can guide classroom questions and additional practice; faculty judgment is needed to understand why an answer was selected. Files stay in your browser.

Return and master it

After the review, try the questions again.

After reading the one-page review, students return to the game to practice the concept. Repair questions revisit the basics, Bridge questions help students apply them, and another round of practice shows how they are doing.
